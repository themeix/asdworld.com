<p>Jen Laviano (<a href="https://twitter.com/JenLaviano">@JenLaviano</a>) has a <a href="https://www.connecticutspecialeducationlawyer.com/occasional-rants/paging-dr-freud/">post</a> up this week that mentions my un-favorite urban legend about autism: that there are parents who get their kids a diagnosis so that they can get &#8220;extra help&#8221; from their school district.</p>
<p>Jen talks about having discussions with parents, friends, relatives, teachers, etc. to explain the value of special education laws and our goals of educating kids who fall into these statutory groups.</p>
<p>Jen, it&#8217;s not just friends and acquaintances who ask strange questions with missing premises. The anguish some parents feel is simply incomprehensible for parents of typical kids. I have colleagues, some of whom are also lawyers, and who I know/believe to be generally kind people.</p>
<p>Yet they will repeat (more than once) this urban legend about parents wanting to have their children classified so that their kids will get extra services in school. I have yet to meet one of these parents, of course. I doubt that the benefits of &#8220;extra help&#8221; will ever overcome the stigma of &#8220;special ed&#8221; in any parent so focused on the outcome for her typical child.</p>
<p class="note">If you don&#8217;t think &#8220;special ed&#8221; <a href="https://twitter.com/dawsonink/statuses/9241834940">carries</a> any <a href="https://twitter.com/Faydetoblack/status/9246114071">stigma</a>, search twitter for #specialed and see what pops up from people outside of advocates, lawyers, and parents. These folks had to type that in on purpose.</p>
<p>I gently remind them that while that is a hypothetical possibility, it&#8217;s easy enough to identify far too many children whose deficits are so great that it absolutely interferes with their daily existence. &#8220;Like my son,&#8221; I say.</p>
<p>Perhaps that&#8217;s one of many &#8220;next jobs&#8221; on this front: identify these parents so ignorant of the label that they will push NT kids into our <a href="https://sed.sagepub.com/content/43/4/236.abstract">wonderfully</a> effective special education system. Because then I can send all my clients&#8217; kids to that school district where special ed works so well.</p>
